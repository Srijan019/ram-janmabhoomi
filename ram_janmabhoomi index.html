Ram Janmabhoomi

राम जन्मभूमि — the birthplace of Lord Rama

A site in Ayodhya at the centre of India's longest-running religious, legal and political story — from an ancient belief to a modern temple.

S C R O L L

Ram Janmabhoomi means “the birthplace of Lord Rama.” It refers to a site in Ayodhya, Uttar Pradesh, traditionally believed by Hindus to be the birthplace of Lord Rama — a central figure in the Ramayana and an important deity in Hinduism.

How the dispute unfolded

16th century

The Babri Masjid, a mosque, was built at the site. Hindu groups long claimed it stood over the remains of an earlier temple marking Rama's birthplace — a claim that fuelled decades of religious and legal dispute, and became a major issue in Indian politics and society.

1992

The Babri Masjid was demolished by a large gathering of activists, triggering widespread communal violence across India. A lengthy legal process followed.

November 2019

The Supreme Court of India unanimously ruled that the disputed site be given for the construction of a Ram temple, and directed that 5 acres of alternative land be provided to the Sunni Central Waqf Board for a mosque.

22 January 2024

Construction of the Ram Mandir culminated in its Pran Pratishtha (consecration ceremony), attended by Prime Minister Narendra Modi and other dignitaries.

Today

Ram Janmabhoomi now stands as both an important religious pilgrimage site for Hindus and a significant landmark in India's modern social, legal and political history.
