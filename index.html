Rama Janmabhoomi

राम जन्मभूमि — the birthplace of Lord Rama

A sacred site in Ayodhya at the heart of Hindu tradition, and the centre of India's longest-running religious, legal and political story.

S C R O L L

Rama Janmabhoomi, meaning "birthplace of Rama," is a sacred site located in Ayodhya, Uttar Pradesh. According to Hindu tradition, Ayodhya is the birthplace of Lord Rama, the central character of the ancient epic Ramayana and an incarnation of Lord Vishnu. Rama is admired for qualities such as courage, truthfulness, responsibility, sacrifice and devotion to dharma. Because of this association, Ayodhya has been a centre of Hindu faith and pilgrimage for centuries.

The sacred city of Ayodhya

Ayodhya sits on the banks of the Sarayu River and is traditionally described as the capital of the ancient Kosala kingdom. According to the Ramayana, Rama was born here to King Dasharatha and Queen Kaushalya. The city is also counted among the Sapta Puri, the seven sacred cities of Hinduism, and its religious importance is renewed each year through festivals connected with Rama — most of all Ram Navami and Diwali.

A site with a complex history

In the 16th century, the Babri Masjid was constructed at the location during the Mughal period. Many Hindus maintained that this was the birthplace of Rama and that a Hindu religious structure had stood there earlier. Over time, these competing claims grew into a dispute over ownership and worship at the site.

How the dispute unfolded

16th century

The Babri Masjid was built during the Mughal period, on land many Hindus believed held the remains of an earlier Rama temple.

December 1949

Idols of Ram Lalla, representing Rama in his childhood form, appeared inside the Babri Masjid. Authorities restricted access to the structure, and Hindu and Muslim parties filed several legal cases over ownership and worship rights.

6 December 1992

The Babri Masjid was demolished by a large crowd of Hindu activists, after the issue had grown increasingly prominent in Indian public life through the 1980s. The demolition led to serious communal tensions and violence across several parts of India.

2003

The Archaeological Survey of India excavated the site on the Allahabad High Court's orders, and reported evidence of a large pre-existing structure beneath the remains of the mosque — findings that became part of the evidence in later legal proceedings.

2010

The Allahabad High Court proposed dividing the disputed land among three parties. The ruling was appealed to the Supreme Court of India.

9 November 2019

A five-judge Constitution Bench of the Supreme Court delivered a unanimous judgment, awarding the land for the construction of a Ram temple and directing a trust be formed to manage it. It also ordered that five acres of alternative land be given to the Sunni Central Waqf Board for a mosque.

2020

The Shri Ram Janmabhoomi Teerth Kshetra trust was established to oversee construction of the Ram Mandir.

5 August 2020

The Bhoomi Pujan, or foundation ceremony, took place. The temple was designed mainly in the traditional Nagara style of Hindu architecture, featuring large pillars, stone carvings and sculptures rooted in tradition.

22 January 2024

The Pran Pratishtha ceremony of Ram Lalla was performed at the newly built Ram Mandir, followed by millions of devotees across India and abroad.

Ayodhya today

The Ram Mandir has transformed Ayodhya into a major religious tourism destination. Roads, transport links, accommodation and other infrastructure have been expanded to serve the growing number of pilgrims and visitors arriving each year.

Legacy

Today, Rama Janmabhoomi is a religious, historical and cultural landmark of India. For millions of devotees, it represents faith and devotion to Lord Rama. Its long history also demonstrates the complex relationship between religion, archaeology, law, politics and society — a relationship the Ram Mandir now carries forward as part of both India's ancient traditions and its modern history.
